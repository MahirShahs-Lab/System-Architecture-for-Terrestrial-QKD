# National QKD Terrestrial Network Architecture

Internship contributing to the design of a national Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) terrestrial network architecture, part of a broader European effort to build quantum-secured communication infrastructure. University of Würzburg academic supervision: Prof. Dr. Andreas Nüchter.

> **Note on confidentiality:** This project was completed under an NDA with the European Commission. General background, QKD theory, and my role (below) are safe to share. Specific architecture diagrams, the detailed functional architecture model, vendor/device specifics, and other restricted project details are **not** included here or in any uploaded files, and should not be published.

## Overview

QKD uses quantum mechanics (rather than computational complexity) to distribute encryption keys with security that's provably resistant to interception, including future attacks from quantum computers. This work was part of a national initiative to build a quantum-secured communication infrastructure, combining a terrestrial fiber network with a future satellite segment, as part of a wider European push to deploy quantum-secured infrastructure across member states.

The project I supported is a multi-year national contribution to this effort, involving a consortium of academic, industrial, and governmental partners, aiming to design, develop, procure, and deploy an experimental national QKD network with the goal of it evolving into a fully operational quantum-secured communication infrastructure.

## My Role

My contributions to the project's analysis and development phases included:

- Preliminary research into QKD principles, protocols (BB84, E91, MDI-QKD), and practical implementation challenges (transmission range, integration with classical networks, scalability)
- Defining use cases and scenarios for the national quantum communication infrastructure
- Researching international standards for QKD architecture: ITU and ETSI specifications
- Surveying and evaluating available commercial QKD devices and their characteristics
- Contributing to a functional architecture model for the national QKD system (network topology, Key Management System design considerations), shared with project consortium members, though full details remain under NDA restriction

## What I'd highlight

- Direct exposure to how national-level secure communication infrastructure is scoped, standardized, and architected, bridging telecom/satellite engineering with emerging quantum technology
- Worked alongside an international, multi-stakeholder consortium (industry, research institutions, government) on a live, EU-funded infrastructure project
